# METHOD: Rental Housing Law Navigator

**Not legal advice.** A prototype built from the fixed participant corpus (pack v5). Default
query date 2026-10-01; every answer shows its as-of date, citation and retrieval date.

## Problem
For each of the 500 sample addresses (CA, NJ, MA): which state and city housing rules in six
categories apply on a query date, and how do the five supplied change cases (T1 to T5) move
the answer? Every answer must trace to an exact sentence of source text.

## Pipeline: extract > verify > resolve > apply > track
1. **Ingest and chunk** (deterministic). The 54 manifest documents with text are read with
   their `SOURCE`/`RETRIEVED` headers checked against the manifest, then cut at headings.
2. **Extract** (the only LLM step for rules). Claude Opus returns structured candidates per
   chunk: the rule fields, a verbatim `quoted_span`, the verbatim effective-date phrase and
   anchor, and coverage predicates over a fixed fact vocabulary. Calls are cached on disk by
   (text sha, chunk, chunker version, prompt version, model), so reruns are reproducible.
3. **Verify** (deterministic gates). A candidate is rejected unless its jurisdiction is a
   manifest jurisdiction and its quote is found in the source text. Numbers in the rule must
   occur near the quote; predicates must use known facts. Failures lower confidence and set
   `needs_review` with a reason. `source_doc_id`, `source_url` and retrieval date always come
   from the manifest, never from the model.
4. **Resolve** each address: the Census Geocoder point, then the incorporated place that
   contains it is the legal city (postal city is not the legal city). NJ and MA also check
   the county subdivision. Units and year built come from the address file; ambiguous NJ
   MOD-IV unit codes give no value rather than a guess.
5. **Apply** (plain Python, unit-tested). Per address and date: status on that date, then
   three-valued coverage (true / false / unknown with the missing fact named), then
   precedence. Results: `applies`, `unknown`, `superseded`, `not_yet_effective`, `pending`;
   rules that don't apply are left out.
6. **Track** T1 to T5: a deterministic matcher maps the organizers' rule ids to ours, then the
   engine runs on the dates each test names. Assertions from `dev/change_tests.json` are checked.

## How invented rules are prevented
- Every stored rule has a quote found verbatim in its document; otherwise it is rejected.
- No rule text, citation, threshold, date or place is typed into code, config or the UI.
- Fail closed: a missing fact gives `unknown` with that fact named, never "does not apply".
- No LLM at answer time. The API serves precomputed JSON and runs only the deterministic
  engine; the exception is adding a new document (`POST /ingest`, `rerun-live`).
- Plain-language sentences are written at build time and dropped unless every number, date
  and amount appears in the quote; wording that helps evade a rule is dropped. The UI then
  shows the quote. Owner wording describes obligations only.

## Dates
The model copies the date words; `dates.py` computes the date with named rules
(`explicit_date`, `first_day_of_nth_month_after`, `days_after`, `ca_default_jan1_next_year`).
Example: the NJ FAIR Act's "first day of the twelfth month next following" enactment,
anchored on "approved July 20, 2026", gives 2027-07-01. A default not stated in the text (the
California January 1 rule, anchored on the bill's own chaptering line) lowers confidence and
flags review. Two sources giving different dates for one law set `conflict_flag`.

## Precedence and conflicts
Precedence comes only from extracted text: a state rule that defers to local rules is
`superseded` where a covering city rule is confirmed, `unknown` ("may be superseded") where it
is not. Interactions the text states but does not resolve flag both rows for human review.
The pending FAIR Act preemption is flagged in T3 for Jersey City and Hoboken addresses.

## Known gaps (said openly)
- **No text for the Jersey City and Hoboken algorithmic bans** (T2) or the MA ballot question
  IP 25-21 (T5). Nothing is invented: T2 lists the addresses whose legal city is Hoboken or
  Jersey City as a boundary-only answer with a "no source text" note, and the rules are absent
  from `rules.json`/`lookups.json`. T5 is empty and no rent cap is reported (M.G.L. c.40P in
  the corpus bars local rent control); IP 25-21 cannot be recorded as `failed` without a
  citable span.
- **Berkeley ch. 13.63 and the LA RSO formula**: the second published effective date is in a
  link-only source, so the two-date conflict cannot be shown from supplied text.
- **Addresses**: a few have no house number or no geocoder match and stay state-only; a
  non-exact geocoder match leaves the city unconfirmed, so city rules there are `unknown`.
  Missing year built or unit count gives `unknown` where coverage depends on it.
- **Values the corpus does not contain** (a 2026 CPI figure, a screening-fee dollar cap) are
  never computed; the formula from the law is shown.

## Adding a jurisdiction
Add its documents through `data/supplement/` (one official page at a time, same header) and
its Census place, then run the pipeline; `python -m navigator ingest-doc <file>` does one
document incrementally. No code changes.
